# Intellectual Property and Attribution

**Status:** Informational only. Not legal advice. Have a qualified IP lawyer review before any agreement is signed.

## 1. Attribution

- Keep dated, versioned documentation of this proposal (commit history or a timestamped archive).
- Use a private repository until you approve publication.
- Signed commits can show authorship. Confirm your signing setup before the first commit.
- Use a "Originated by Federico Croletti" notice in the documents, limited to this specific proposal.

## 2. What can be protected

| Item | Protection | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Written documents | Copyright (automatic) | Protects the wording and structure, not the underlying ideas |
| Original source code | Copyright (automatic) | Does not protect the idea of recognition through MCP |
| Genuinely novel technical method | Patent (possible) | Only if novel, non-obvious, and industrially applicable. Requires a prior-art search and patent counsel. File before public disclosure |
| Name and brand | Trademark (optional) | Check availability before using a product name |

## 3. What cannot be claimed

- Music recognition itself, MCP itself, ShazamKit, or any existing provider API.
- Exclusive ownership of general ideas that already exist (see [COMPETITIVE-ANALYSIS.md](./COMPETITIVE-ANALYSIS.md)).

## 4. Europe

- Patent novelty can be lost by public disclosure. Do not disclose technical details publicly or in unprotected outreach before reviewing options.
- For copyright, no registration is required in the EU, but evidence of creation date helps.
- Confidentiality: use a mutual non-disclosure agreement (NDA) before sharing technical details with any company.

## 5. Conditions before any external submission

- Do not accept terms that waive attribution, confidentiality, or compensation without your explicit review.
- Do not transfer IP or sign an agreement without legal review.
- Check each company's external idea-submission policy; many decline unsolicited ideas or require a waiver. Do not submit under such a waiver without your approval.

## 6. Repository visibility

Recommendation: keep the repository **private** until you approve publication. Public release can happen later, after a patent decision and after confirming that no third-party or licensed content is included.
